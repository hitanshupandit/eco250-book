## Coming as we teach them

Chapters 3 to 10 fill in the first half of the course — spread and standardising,
covariance and correlation, the probability rules, Bayes, random variables, the
binomial, the normal, and the trio of $t$, $\chi^2$ and $F$. Chapters 15 to 18 cover
two-sample comparisons, ANOVA, categorical data and regression.

Chapters 11 to 14 were written first because they are what we are doing now.

## How to read it

Read with a pen. Every worked example is set out the way I want an answer written on
a problem set, so cover the solution and try it first. Reading a worked example and
producing one are different skills, and only the second is any use when there is a
clock running.

The examples repeat on purpose. The same commute data runs through Chapters 13 and
14; the same gym example appears in 12 and 14. You are meant to notice that a new
tool is answering a different question about data you already understand, rather than
spending your attention on a fresh scenario every time.

Stata appears only as it was taught. If you meet a command you have not run in a lab,
that is a mistake and I would like to know about it.

## Accuracy

Every number in the worked examples is computed by scripts rather than typed, and the
Stata figures were produced by running the code. If a number here disagrees with a
number on a lecture slide, **the book is wrong** — tell me and I will fix it.
