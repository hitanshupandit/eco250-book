Regression Toward the Mean

ECON 250 · Chapter 18 · what Galton actually found in 1886

Galton measured the heights of parents and their grown children, looking for a law of inheritance. What he found instead was a pull back toward the average, and he called it regression toward mediocrity. The name later transferred to the method he used to measure it — which is why we call the technique regression, even though the technique does no such thing.

Things to try. Push r to 1. The fitted line lies on the dashed line and nobody moves — no regression to the mean at all. Now pull r to 0: every group's children average the same height, whatever their parents. Regression toward the mean is not a law of biology. It is simply what r below 1 looks like.

2. So why doesn't everyone end up the same height?

This is the objection Galton worried about. If tall parents have shorter children and short parents have taller ones, generation after generation, should the whole population not collapse to a single height?

Near enough identical, at any value of r. The distribution is not shrinking. What regresses is the average of a group picked out for being extreme, not the population. The children of tall parents are shorter than their parents, but plenty of tall children are born to ordinary parents, and they refill the top of the distribution. Selection, not shrinkage.

Why this matters beyond heights. Take the ten students who scored highest on one test and they will usually do worse on the next — not because anything went wrong, but because an unusually high score takes some luck and luck does not repeat. The same arithmetic explains the athlete who has a quieter season after making the magazine cover, and why a policy brought in right after an unusually bad year tends to look effective. Whenever a group is selected for being extreme and then measured again, expect movement toward the mean before you reach for a cause.

Two different things share one word. Regression toward the mean is the phenomenon on this page. Regression is the method in Chapter 18 — fitting a line by least squares. Nothing in least squares regresses toward anything.
